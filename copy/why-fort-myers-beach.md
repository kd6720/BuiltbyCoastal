## Section: Infrastructure — Recovery & Resilience

### Headline
The foundation is stronger than ever.

### Body
Since Hurricane Ian, Fort Myers Beach has undergone one of the most thorough infrastructure rebuilds on the Gulf Coast. These aren't plans or proposals — they're active projects with completion dates.

**Estero Boulevard Repair Program.** A $9 million roadway and drainage improvement project, currently 25% complete, with full completion expected by Fall 2027. The main artery of the island is being rebuilt to higher standards.

**San Carlos Boulevard Corridor.** A $13 million project introducing smart traffic signals and a seasonal transit lane — dramatically improving access to and from the island for both residents and visitors.

**Water and Sewer Systems.** Fully restored and operational. Reliable utilities are in place — no uncertainty, no temporary solutions.

**Storm-Hardened Town Hall.** Opened September 2025. A visible symbol of the island's commitment to resilience and long-term recovery.

**FEMA CRS Class 5 Rating (2026).** This is significant. Fort Myers Beach has achieved a Community Rating System Class 5 designation, which delivers a 25% discount on flood insurance premiums island-wide. For property owners, this is a durable, structural cost advantage — not a temporary incentive.

**Community Connectivity.** The free seasonal trolley and tram service has been restored. The mooring field has reopened with 19 moorings for vessels up to 85 feet. The public library reopened in March 2025. These are the signals of a functioning, vibrant community — not a recovery zone.

## Section: Development Pipeline — Active & Approved

### Headline
$200 million says the market believes in this island.

### Body
Private capital is flowing into Fort Myers Beach at scale. The development pipeline isn't speculative — projects are open, under construction, and approved.

**Margaritaville Beach Resort.** A $200 million, 254-room resort that opened in December 2023. Refinanced in 2026 based on strong net operating income — the market has already validated this investment.

**New Fishing Pier.** A $17.7 million rebuild, 70% longer than the original pier. Completion expected between 2027-2028. The pier is both a community landmark and a tourism draw — it anchors the Times Square district.

**Sunset Beach Resort at Times Square.** A $13 million mixed-use development that broke ground in July 2026 and is expected to open mid-2027. New rooms, new retail, new energy in the heart of the beach district.

**Approved Lodging Pipeline.** Arches Bayfront (263 rooms approved). Silver Sands Resort expansion (60 rooms). Pink Shell expansion (40 rooms). In total, approximately 584 hotel rooms and units are approved and moving through the development pipeline.

These aren't distant possibilities. They're active projects backed by institutional capital and hospitality operators who have done their homework on this market.

## Section: Why This Matters for Investors

### Headline
Scarcity. Demand. Infrastructure. Timing.

### Body

**Scarcity.** Fort Myers Beach is a barrier island. There is no more land being made. Buildable lots are finite, and post-Ian construction standards mean new builds meet higher codes than anything that existed before.

**Demand.** 3.27 million visitors came to the area in FY24-25. Tourism is not a theory here — it's the economy. And hotel rooms remain 41% below pre-Ian levels, which means vacation rentals fill a genuine supply gap.

**Infrastructure.** Roads, water, sewer, transit, flood mitigation — all rebuilt or actively under construction. The infrastructure risk that plagues emerging markets doesn't apply here. The work is being done.

**Timing.** The rebuild is happening now. The development pipeline is active now. The FEMA Class 5 rating is in effect now. Investors who wait for "more certainty" will find themselves competing with the Margaritavilles and Pink Shells of the world for what's left.
