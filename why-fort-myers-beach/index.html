Infrastructure — Recovery & Resilience

The foundation is stronger than ever.

Since Hurricane Ian, Fort Myers Beach has undergone one of the most thorough infrastructure rebuilds on the Gulf Coast. These aren't plans or proposals — they're active projects with completion dates.

Estero Boulevard Repair

A $9M roadway and drainage program, currently 25% complete, with full completion expected Fall 2027. The main artery of the island, rebuilt to higher standards.

San Carlos Boulevard Corridor

A $13M project introducing smart traffic signals and a seasonal transit lane — dramatically improving access to and from the island.

Water & Sewer Systems

Fully restored and operational. Reliable utilities are in place — no uncertainty, no temporary solutions.

Storm-Hardened Town Hall

Opened September 2025 — a visible symbol of the island's commitment to resilience and long-term recovery.

FEMA CRS Class 5 Rating

A 2026 Community Rating System Class 5 designation delivers a 25% flood-insurance discount island-wide — a durable, structural cost advantage, not a temporary incentive.

Community Connectivity

The free seasonal trolley and tram are restored. The mooring field reopened with 19 moorings for vessels up to 85 feet. The public library reopened March 2025.

Development Pipeline — Active & Approved

$200 million says the market believes in this island.

Private capital is flowing into Fort Myers Beach at scale. The pipeline isn't speculative — projects are open, under construction, and approved.

Open · 2023

Margaritaville Beach Resort

A $200M, 254-room resort that opened December 2023 and refinanced in 2026 on strong net operating income — the market has already validated this investment.

Under construction · 2027–28

New Fishing Pier

A $17.7M rebuild, 70% longer than the original. Both a community landmark and a tourism draw — it anchors the Times Square district.

Broke ground · 2026

Sunset Beach Resort

A $13M mixed-use development at Times Square, expected to open mid-2027 — new rooms, new retail, new energy in the heart of the beach district.

Approved lodging pipeline: Arches Bayfront (263 rooms), Silver Sands Resort expansion (60 rooms), and Pink Shell expansion (40 rooms) — roughly 584 hotel rooms and units approved and moving through development, backed by institutional capital and hospitality operators who have done their homework.

Why This Matters for Investors

Scarcity. Demand. Infrastructure. Timing.

Scarcity

Fort Myers Beach is a barrier island. There is no more land being made. Buildable lots are finite, and post-Ian standards mean new builds meet higher codes than anything before.

Demand

3.27 million visitors came to the area in FY24–25. Hotel rooms remain 41% below pre-Ian levels, so vacation rentals fill a genuine supply gap.

Infrastructure

Roads, water, sewer, transit, flood mitigation — all rebuilt or actively under construction. The infrastructure risk that plagues emerging markets doesn't apply here.

Timing

The rebuild is happening now. Investors who wait for "more certainty" will find themselves competing with the Margaritavilles and Pink Shells for what's left.
